# Biography

Jordan Christopher Lyles is a medical student at Rosalind Franklin University of Medicine and Science with a background in biomedical engineering from Purdue University. His work spans orthopaedic surgery, rehabilitation engineering, biomedical signal processing, medical device development, and artificial intelligence for clinical decision support.

Prior to medical school, Jordan worked as an Orthopaedic Medical Assistant while conducting biomedical engineering research focused on wearable rehabilitation technologies, computational medicine, musculoskeletal biomechanics, and medical device development. His engineering experience includes research at Baxter International, undergraduate biomechanics research at Purdue University, and multiple design projects involving rehabilitation technology, fuzzy logic, and biomedical instrumentation.

His long-term goal is to become an academic orthopaedic surgeon who integrates clinical care, engineering, and translational research to improve patient outcomes.
